What Is Attention Span?

Attention span is the amount of time you can concentrate on a task without becoming distracted. It is not a single ability but a family of related processes including selective, sustained, divided, and executive attention.

Key Takeaway

Your attention span is not 8 seconds (that statistic is fabricated). Sustained attention on a single task typically lasts 20-45 minutes before performance declines. Attention is a trainable family of skills — selective, sustained, divided, and executive — each serving different cognitive functions.

The Four Types of Attention

Attention is not a single thing. Cognitive psychology identifies several distinct types, each supported by different neural networks:

  • Selective attention: The ability to focus on one stimulus while filtering out distractions. This is what you use when listening to a conversation in a noisy restaurant (the cocktail party effect).
  • Sustained attention (vigilance): The ability to maintain focus on a task over an extended period. Air traffic controllers, long-distance drivers, and students in lectures rely heavily on sustained attention.
  • Divided attention: The ability to process multiple information streams simultaneously. True parallel processing is extremely limited — what feels like multitasking is usually rapid task-switching.
  • Executive attention: The ability to resolve conflict between competing stimuli or responses. Measured by tasks like the Stroop test, where you must override an automatic response.

The 8-Second Attention Span Myth

A widely cited claim states that human attention span dropped from 12 seconds in 2000 to 8 seconds in 2015 — less than a goldfish. This statistic originated from a 2015 Microsoft Canada report, but the original source it cited does not exist. No published study has ever measured human attention span at 8 seconds. The BBC, the Associated Press, and multiple researchers have debunked this claim.

In reality, sustained attention on laboratory tasks begins declining measurably after 20-30 minutes, with significant performance drops after 45 minutes. The vigilance decrement — the gradual decline in detection accuracy over a sustained attention task — is a universal human phenomenon documented across thousands of studies since Mackworth (1948).

How Attention Is Measured

Clinical and research assessments of attention include:

  • Continuous Performance Test (CPT): Present rapid stimuli; the subject responds to targets and withholds responses to non-targets. Measures sustained attention and response inhibition over 10-20 minutes.
  • Sustained Attention to Response Task (SART): Similar to CPT but uses a go/no-go format. Performance decline over time quantifies the vigilance decrement.
  • Attention Network Test (ANT): Developed by Fan et al. (2002), separately measures alerting, orienting, and executive attention networks in a single 20-minute session.

How to Improve Attention Span

Research supports several approaches to improving attention:

  • Mindfulness meditation: Eight weeks of MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction) produces measurable improvements in sustained attention (Jha et al., 2007). Even brief daily sessions of 10-15 minutes show benefits after 4 weeks.
  • Aerobic exercise: Regular cardiovascular exercise is one of the most robust attention enhancers available. Hillman et al. (2008) found exercise improves executive attention across all age groups.
  • Cognitive training: Targeted attention training games — especially CPT-style sustained attention tasks and Stroop-style executive attention tasks — improve performance on trained and related attention measures.
  • Environmental design: Eliminating notifications, using single-task workflows, and working in distraction-free environments reduces the attentional cost of task-switching.
Research Note: A study by Mark et al. (2008) at UC Irvine found that after an interruption, it takes an average of 23 minutes and 15 seconds to fully return to the original task. This is not an attention span limit — it is the cost of context-switching.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Is the 8-second attention span statistic real?

    No. This statistic has been thoroughly debunked. It originated from a 2015 Microsoft Canada report that cited a non-existent source. No published peer-reviewed study supports an 8-second human attention span. Sustained attention on laboratory tasks typically lasts 20-45 minutes before measurable decline.

  • How is attention span measured?

    Attention is measured using standardized tasks like the Continuous Performance Test (CPT) and Sustained Attention to Response Task (SART). These tests last 10-20 minutes and measure accuracy, response time, and the rate of performance decline over time (the vigilance decrement).

  • Does ADHD reduce attention span?

    ADHD significantly impairs sustained and executive attention, but not all types equally. Individuals with ADHD can hyperfocus on highly engaging tasks while struggling with routine or less stimulating activities. The core deficit in ADHD is in attentional regulation — controlling where attention goes — rather than a simple reduction in span.
