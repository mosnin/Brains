How to Play

A stream of digits (0–9) will appear on screen one at a time. Your only job is to press SPACE (or tap the RESPOND button) when the digit 0 appears. Do not respond to any other digit. The digit 0 is rare — appearing only 6–20% of the time depending on the level — so resist the urge to respond impulsively.

  • Hit — correctly responding when 0 appears.
  • Miss — failing to respond when 0 appears.
  • False Alarm — responding when a non-0 digit appears.

Your score combines hits and correct rejections. Minimise both misses and false alarms to score above 80% and pass the level.

What It Measures

The Vigilance Task measures sustained attention — the ability to maintain focused readiness over an extended period and detect infrequent, critical signals. It also probes inhibitory control (suppressing responses to non-targets) and signal detection sensitivity (distinguishing targets from distractors).

Key cognitive constructs assessed:

  • Sustained attention / vigilance
  • Response inhibition
  • Signal detection (d-prime)
  • Impulsivity monitoring via false alarm rate

Research

Vigilance research traces back to Norman MacKworth's 1948 "clock test," developed to assess Royal Air Force radar operators who needed to detect rare signal deflections across long watches. MacKworth demonstrated the now-classic vigilance decrement — a reliable drop in detection performance after about 20–30 minutes on task.

Modern variants, including the Continuous Performance Test (CPT), are used clinically to assess ADHD, traumatic brain injury, and other conditions affecting sustained attention. Signal Detection Theory (Green & Swets, 1966) provides the mathematical framework for separating perceptual sensitivity (d') from response bias (criterion), enabling a richer picture than raw accuracy alone.

Lower target frequency (as seen in higher BrainForge levels) increases task demand and sharpens the vigilance decrement effect, making it a more sensitive probe of attentional resources.

FAQ

Why are the digits shown so briefly?

Stimulus duration and interstimulus interval (ISI) are core parameters in vigilance research. Shorter durations and faster pacing increase cognitive load and make sustained responding harder. At higher levels the ISI drops to 500 ms — close to the limits of human simple reaction time — requiring tight attentional focus with no lapses.

What is a good false alarm rate?

In clinical CPT norms, healthy adults typically produce false alarm rates below 5–10%. A high false alarm rate suggests impulsive responding — acting before fully processing the stimulus. The vigilance score on BrainForge weights false alarms into the final percentage, so keeping them low is just as important as catching every target.

Does performance on this test predict real-world attention?

Vigilance tasks have moderate ecological validity. They correlate with driving performance, air-traffic control accuracy, and occupational tasks requiring sustained monitoring. Clinically, CPT scores are used as supplementary evidence in ADHD assessment. However, like all isolated cognitive tasks, they measure a slice of a multidimensional construct and should not be used in isolation for diagnostic conclusions.
