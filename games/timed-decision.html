How to Play

Each trial shows a rule at the top (for example, "Is this number greater than 5?") and a stimulus in the centre — a number, letter, shape, or word. Your job is to respond as quickly as possible.

  • Click Yes (or press Y) if the stimulus satisfies the rule.
  • Click No (or press N) if it does not.
  • For Odd/Even levels, press O for Odd or E for Even.
  • Each level contains 20–30 items. Score 80% or more to pass and unlock the next level.
  • Later levels mix multiple rules, so read the rule on each trial carefully.

What It Measures

The Timed Decision Task is a choice reaction time paradigm. Unlike a simple reaction test where you react to any signal, here you must process the stimulus, apply a rule, and generate a correct binary response — all under time pressure.

The task taps processing speed, working memory (holding the rule in mind), and decision-making efficiency. As levels progress and rules become more cognitively demanding (e.g., prime number detection, mixed rules), the task also engages executive control.

Research Background

Hick's Law (1952) established a foundational principle: reaction time increases logarithmically with the number of possible choices. The Timed Decision Task operationalises this by progressively introducing more cognitively demanding rules, requiring finer discrimination at each step.

Choice reaction time is one of the most robust predictors of general intelligence (Jensen, 1982) and is used in clinical neuropsychology to detect processing deficits associated with ADHD, traumatic brain injury, and age-related cognitive decline. Research by Deary et al. (2001) confirmed that simple and choice reaction time correlate significantly with psychometric IQ (r ≈ −0.31 to −0.49), with the relationship strengthening under time pressure.

The task's progressive structure — from simple greater-than judgements to prime number detection to mixed-rule trials — mirrors classic decision-making paradigms used in cognitive science laboratories, making it both an engaging training tool and a sensitive performance measure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good response time on the Timed Decision Task?

For easy levels (e.g., "greater than 5"), average response times under 700 ms with 95%+ accuracy reflect strong processing speed. For harder rule types such as prime detection, under 1,100 ms is considered fast. Expert-level performance (Levels 29–30) typically requires consistent sub-600 ms responses across mixed rules to achieve the 80% pass threshold.

Why are prime number questions included?

Prime detection is cognitively demanding because it cannot be resolved by a single rule — the brain must implicitly rule out all smaller divisors. This engages working memory and arithmetic fluency under time pressure, making it an excellent probe of higher-order processing speed. The difficulty contrast between simple (greater than 5) and complex (prime) tasks also illustrates Hick's Law directly: more decision complexity means longer reaction time.

How does the mixed-rule format in later levels work?

From Level 25 onward, the rule changes on every trial. A trial might ask "Is this prime?" followed by "Does this word start with a vowel?" The rule is always displayed above the stimulus, so you must read it before responding. This design measures cognitive flexibility — your ability to switch mental sets rapidly — in addition to raw decision speed.
