How to Play

A sequence of digits will appear one at a time in the center of the screen. Once all digits have been shown, type them back in reverse order — last digit first, first digit last.

A prominent "REVERSE ORDER" reminder is always visible during the input phase. Use the on-screen numpad or your keyboard. You have 5 trials per level and must get at least 4 correct to advance. The challenge increases as spans grow longer and display times shorten.

What It Measures

The backward digit span test goes beyond simple storage. Recalling digits in reverse requires you to hold information in working memory while simultaneously manipulating it — a process that heavily engages the central executive and prefrontal cortex.

This makes it a more sensitive measure of executive function than the forward version. It is included as a subtest in the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS-IV) and is used clinically to detect working memory impairments in ADHD, schizophrenia, traumatic brain injury, and early-stage Alzheimer's disease.

Research

Numerous studies have demonstrated that backward digit span is more strongly correlated with fluid intelligence and executive function than forward digit span. Engle et al. (1999) showed that working memory capacity — measured by tasks requiring active manipulation like backward span — predicts performance on complex reasoning tasks independently of processing speed.

Reynolds (1997) established normative data showing that backward spans are typically 1–2 digits shorter than forward spans across all age groups. A clinically significant discrepancy between forward and backward span can indicate selective impairment of the executive control system, making this test a valuable diagnostic tool in neuropsychological assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the digit span backward test measure?

The digit span backward test measures working memory capacity and executive function. Unlike the forward version, it requires you to actively manipulate information — holding a sequence in mind while reversing it — which engages the central executive component of working memory and the prefrontal cortex.

How is digit span backward different from digit span forward?

The forward test measures passive verbal storage (phonological loop), while the backward test requires active manipulation: you must hold the sequence, mentally reverse it, and then recall it. Backward spans are typically 1–2 digits shorter than forward spans and more sensitive to cognitive impairments affecting executive function.

Is digit span backward used clinically?

Yes. Digit span backward is a subtest of the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS) and Wechsler Memory Scale (WMS). Clinicians use it to assess working memory deficits in conditions including ADHD, schizophrenia, traumatic brain injury, and Alzheimer's disease. A backward span significantly lower than forward span can be clinically meaningful.
