How to Play

A sequence of digits will appear one at a time in the center of the screen. Each digit flashes briefly before disappearing. Once the entire sequence has been shown, type the digits back in the same order they appeared.

Use the on-screen numpad or your keyboard. Press Submit (or Enter) when you are ready. You have 5 trials per level and must get at least 4 correct to advance. Levels increase the number of digits and decrease display time as you progress.

What It Measures

The forward digit span test directly measures verbal short-term memory capacity — specifically the phonological loop component of working memory described in Baddeley and Hitch's influential model. The phonological loop temporarily stores and rehearses spoken and written material.

Your digit span is a reliable indicator of how much information your brain can actively hold in the verbal store. Clinicians use this task as part of full neuropsychological assessments (Wechsler Memory Scale, WAIS-IV) to identify working memory deficits associated with ADHD, traumatic brain injury, and learning disorders.

Research

George Miller's classic 1956 paper "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two" established that most adults can hold 5–9 items in short-term memory, with 7 as the average. Subsequent research using digit span tasks has refined our understanding of chunking, rehearsal strategies, and how memory capacity changes across the lifespan.

Studies by Gathercole and colleagues (2004) demonstrated that digit span performance in children predicts later academic achievement in reading and mathematics, highlighting the foundational role of verbal working memory in learning. Training studies suggest that consistent practice with span tasks can modestly improve phonological loop efficiency and attentional control.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the digit span test?

The digit span test measures verbal short-term memory capacity by presenting a sequence of numbers and asking you to recall them in order. It is a core subtest of the Wechsler Memory Scale and is widely used in clinical and research settings to assess working memory.

What is a normal digit span score?

Most healthy adults can recall between 5 and 9 digits (often cited as Miller's Law: 7 ± 2). A forward digit span of 6–8 is considered average for adults, while spans below 5 may indicate short-term memory difficulties.

Can the digit span test improve memory?

Regular practice with digit span tasks can improve chunking strategies and phonological loop efficiency. While the test primarily measures capacity, consistent training has been associated with modest improvements in working memory performance and attention.
