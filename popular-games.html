Top 5 Most Played

These five games stand apart because they are based on the most well-validated cognitive paradigms in neuroscience research. Each has a clear measurable outcome, adapts to your skill level, and has been shown to produce real cognitive benefits with consistent practice.

#1
Working Memory 30 Levels

Dual N-Back

The most-studied working memory training paradigm. Simultaneously track visual positions and spoken letters N steps back in time. Jaeggi et al. (2008) found that Dual N-Back training transferred to gains in fluid intelligence — making it the most famous cognitive training game in neuroscience.

  • Trains both verbal and visuospatial working memory simultaneously
  • Adaptive difficulty keeps you in the optimal challenge zone
  • More research citations than any other cognitive training task
Play Dual N-Back →
#2
Executive Function 30 Levels

Stroop Color-Word Test

Name the ink color while ignoring the written word. First described by John Ridley Stroop in 1935, this is the gold standard measure of inhibitory control. The conflict between automatic reading and intentional color-naming engages the anterior cingulate cortex and prefrontal inhibition systems.

  • 90 years of research validating its sensitivity to executive function
  • Simple concept, deeply challenging at higher speeds
  • Sensitive to age, fatigue, and cognitive load
Play Stroop Test →
#3
Reaction Time 30 Levels

Reaction Time Test

Measure your raw neural processing speed in milliseconds. Click or tap the moment the stimulus appears. Simple reaction time is one of the oldest and most reliable measures in all of psychology — it correlates with general intelligence, reflects neural conduction velocity, and declines predictably with age.

  • Instant, objective measurement in milliseconds
  • Average adults: ~250 ms; teens: ~220 ms; 60+: ~300 ms
  • Sensitive to fatigue, caffeine, and practice effects
Test Reaction Time →
#4
Working Memory Visual Memory 30 Levels

Corsi Block Test

Watch a sequence of blocks light up, then tap them in the same order. Developed by Philip Corsi in 1972, this is the standard measure of visuospatial working memory span. Average adults recall sequences of 5–6 blocks — test yours and see how you compare.

  • Measures visuospatial working memory span (Corsi span ~5–6)
  • Used in clinical neuropsychological assessment worldwide
  • Directly comparable to digit span for spatial vs verbal memory
Play Corsi Block →
#5
Attention 30 Levels

Flanker Task

Identify the direction of the center arrow while flanking arrows point in the same or opposite direction. Developed by Eriksen & Eriksen (1974), the Flanker Task has become the standard measure of selective attention and conflict monitoring — the ability to focus on relevant stimuli and suppress interfering ones.

  • Quantifies congruency effects — how much distractors slow you down
  • Sensitive to attentional control and executive function development
  • One of the most replicated tasks in cognitive neuroscience
